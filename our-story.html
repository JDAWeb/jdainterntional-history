Our Story

Thirty-two years of working alongside communities

From the shores of a shrinking Aral Sea to the wheat fields of northern Afghanistan and the villages of Iraqi Kurdistan.

Beginnings in Uzbekistan

In 1992, Bob Hedlund moved his family to Tashkent, Uzbekistan. Bob was a mining engineer, trained at the Colorado School of Mines, with more than 17 years in the industry. He and his wife Cathy came to Central Asia as the Soviet Union came apart, and they were drawn to the people of Karakalpakstan, living with the environmental disaster of the drying Aral Sea.

JDA began operating in Uzbekistan in 1993, and in December 1994 the IRS recognized JDA as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. The work grew from humanitarian aid into community development: hand-pump wells in Karakalpakstan, a poultry program for widows and single mothers near Shakhrisabz, micro-credit in Muynak, care for leprosy-affected communities, and solar water disinfection in schools. It spread to the Ferghana Valley, Samarkand, and the provinces bordering Afghanistan.

Beginning in 2005, amid strained relations between the governments of Uzbekistan and the United States, Western NGOs were forced to close. JDA was among the last to lose its registration. It was ordered to close in October 2006 and contested the closure in the courts. Read about the work in Uzbekistan.

Northern Afghanistan, 2001 to 2019

After the fall of the Taliban in 2001, JDA opened an office in Mazar-i-Sharif. Village elders asked for schools, especially for girls, and JDA helped communities build them while delivering food and clothing to families returning home after years of war and drought.

Over the next 18 years the Mazar-i-Sharif office served Balkh, Samangan, Sar-e Pul, Jawzjan and Faryab provinces. JDA ran a research farm and used the Development Center outside the city, tested new crops and two-wheel tractors with farmers, trained university staff, drilled wells, and taught hygiene and nutrition with Afghan women educators. Program activities ended in 2019 because of security conditions and partner budget cuts. Read about the work in Afghanistan.

Since 2008, Bob has overseen JDA's work from Grand Junction, Colorado.

Iraqi Kurdistan, from 2017

In 2017 JDA began working in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. With the Dutch organization ZOA and the University of Duhok, JDA helped families who had returned home after displacement rebuild their livelihoods through cash-for-work, greenhouses, field crops and livestock. Read about the work in Iraqi Kurdistan.

Evacuation and resettlement

When Afghanistan's government fell in August 2021, JDA turned to its own people. With Help the Persecuted, JDA worked to evacuate former staff and at-risk Afghans and their families, and then helped them resettle, including in western Colorado through the Grand Valley Resettlement Program and the ARISE program. Read more, with notes on the figures.

Closing in 2025

JDA International closed its operations in October 2025, after 32 years. In his closing letter, Bob thanked the hundreds of local staff members in Uzbekistan, Afghanistan, and Iraq who were "the heart and soul of JDA's mission," and wrote that the spirit of service will continue through the people and communities JDA served. Read the letter on the home page.

Our mission

JDA believes that all people have unlimited creativity and potential for transformation. In that spirit, JDA joins people and their communities in developing holistic solutions toward a more equitable society, supporting locally integrated solutions in community development, education, health, agriculture, and economic growth.

  1. 1992 A move to Tashkent

    Uzbekistan

    Bob Hedlund, a mining engineer trained at the Colorado School of Mines, moves his family to Tashkent, Uzbekistan, shortly after the Soviet Union dissolves.

    Source: JDA records

  2. 1993 JDA begins work in Uzbekistan

    Uzbekistan

    Joint Development Associates International begins operating in Uzbekistan, first in humanitarian aid and then in longer-term community development.

    Source: JDA records

  3. 1994 U.S. nonprofit status

    United States

    In December 1994 the IRS grants JDA tax-exempt status as a 501(c)(3) organization (EIN 84-1286934).

    Source: JDA records; IRS letter of May 20, 1999 confirms public charity status

  4. 1996 Computer center in Jizzak

    Uzbekistan

    JDA opens a computer training center in Jizzak, later transferred to local management.

    Source: JDA website, 2003

  5. 1999 Poultry for widows and single mothers

    Uzbekistan

    A poultry program near Shakhrisabz starts with seven families. By 2003 it reaches about 160 families in 12 villages, most of them headed by women.

    Source: JDA projects page, 2003

  6. 2001 An office in Mazar-i-Sharif

    Afghanistan

    After the fall of the Taliban, JDA opens an office in Mazar-i-Sharif to serve the northern provinces.

    Source: JDA "Who We Are" page, 2016

  7. 2002 Food for returning families

    Afghanistan

    Working from Termez across the Amu Darya as a World Food Programme partner, JDA delivers about 5,000 metric tons of food to more than 20,000 families.

    Source: JDA projects page, 2003

  8. 2003 Food for work and new homes

    Afghanistan

    With Food for the Hungry, a food-for-work program repairs roads and canals and digs shallow wells in Balkh and Jawzjan. With Habitat for Humanity, JDA helps build more than 200 homes.

    Source: JDA projects page, 2003

  9. 2004 Sultan Ibrahim School dedicated

    Afghanistan

    The Sultan Ibrahim School, about an hour and a half from Mazar-i-Sharif, is dedicated on June 16, 2004. A second floor is finished in August 2006.

    Source: JDA school dedication page, 2004; JDA home page, 2007

  10. 2005 Two-wheel tractors and SODIS

    Afghanistan and Uzbekistan

    JDA begins its long work with two-wheel tractors in northern Afghanistan. In Uzbekistan, the Ministry of Health approves expansion of JDA's solar water disinfection (SODIS) program to five regions.

    Source: JDA two-wheel tractor story; JDA SODIS story, August 2005

  11. 2006 Forced closure in Uzbekistan

    Uzbekistan

    Western NGOs in Uzbekistan are forced to close beginning in 2005. JDA is among the last to lose its registration; it is ordered to close in October 2006 and contests the closure in the courts.

    Source: JDA timeline; JDA home page, 2007

  12. 2007 Wheat into rice stubble

    Afghanistan

    Under USAID's AWATT project, a JDA mechanization specialist uses a two-wheel tractor to plant wheat into rice stubble in Kaldar district, starting a wave of private tractor purchases. OHT, JDA's sister company, begins importing the tractors early in the year; it imports 494 in all.

    Source: JDA two-wheel tractor story; OHT company profile; tractor total from JDA records

  13. 2008 Leading from Grand Junction

    United States

    Bob Hedlund oversees the work from Grand Junction, Colorado. In Afghanistan, JDA trains farmers in melon fly control with IFDC.

    Source: JDA history page, 2016; JDA melon fly report, 2008

  14. 2009 Water, sanitation and hygiene

    Afghanistan

    JDA's WASH program begins: wells with hand pumps, biosand filters, and hygiene training led by Afghan women educators. In all, 30,000 women are trained in sanitation and hygiene.

    Source: JDA community health page

  15. 2013 BLiSS for women

    Afghanistan

    BLiSS (Birth Life Saving Skills) begins teaching women basic life-saving skills for pregnancy, birth and newborn care. More than 1,300 women take part by May 2018.

    Source: JDA BLiSS materials, 2018

  16. 2014 RADP-North

    Afghanistan

    JDA becomes an implementer in USAID's five-year Regional Agricultural Development Program-North, training wheat farmers in Baghlan, Balkh, Jawzjan and Samangan.

    Source: JDA RADP-North page; RADP-North fact sheet, 2014

  17. 2017 Iraqi Kurdistan

    Iraqi Kurdistan

    JDA begins work in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq and registers there.

    Source: JDA records; JDA Kurdistan summary

  18. 2018 RIVAL with ZOA

    Iraqi Kurdistan

    With ZOA and funding from the U.S. State Department's Bureau of Population, Refugees, and Migration, JDA starts RIVAL, a livelihoods program for returnees, displaced families and host communities.

    Source: JDA Kurdistan summary; annual report 2019

  19. 2019 Afghanistan programs end

    Afghanistan

    RADP-North ends in May 2019. Because of security conditions and partner budget cuts, JDA ends its program activities in Afghanistan after more than 18 years.

    Source: Annual report 2019

  20. 2021 Evacuation

    Afghanistan

    From August 2021, JDA and Help the Persecuted work to evacuate former JDA staff and at-risk Afghans. About 600 associates and family members are evacuated.

    Source: JDA GVRP page, 2025; see the notes on the Afghanistan page

  21. 2022 Grand Valley Resettlement Program

    United States

    JDA's Grand Valley Resettlement Program begins welcoming Afghan families to western Colorado. In December 2022 it is approved as a community partner of the U.S. refugee program through Lutheran Immigration and Refugee Service.

    Source: JDA GVRP page, 2025

  22. 2023 ARISE announced

    United States

    JDA and GVRP announce ARISE (Afghan Resettlement, Integration, Stabilization, and Empowerment) in Grand Junction, described in 2024 as "set to begin in December 2023".

    Source: JDA home page, 2024

  23. 2025 Closure

    All

    JDA International closes operations in October 2025 after 32 years. This site preserves its history.

    Source: JDA closure notice, 2025
